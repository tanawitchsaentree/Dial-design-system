# Concept — dial

dial is the face of a household appliance, drawn on a screen. People at home use it on a phone or a laptop to turn the heating down, dim a lamp, see whether the washer is done, or skip a track. They look at it for a few seconds and then go back to their lives, so every unit (a card, a sheet, a screen) behaves like one small appliance in the Braun / Ulm tradition. It has a warm-white matte plastic body and one display that says the state. A perforated grille shows where something is happening, and one orange part is the control you turn or press. The rest is grey ink on plastic. Its source is Dieter Rams' ten principles and his motto "Weniger, aber besser", less but better. It must never become a retro costume (fake logos, fake plastic grain, grime), never use the Braun name or mark (dial has its own wordmark), never spread orange across the screen, never set light grey on light grey below 4.5:1, and never swell into a bloated round toy.

### P1 One loud thing per unit
Rams asks that good design be unobtrusive. Each unit has exactly one primary control, and only that control is orange. Everything else is ink, plastic or display. **Therefore** space: the loud control gets the largest clear area in the unit and sits where the hand expects it (lower or right of the display). shape: the loud control is the one round or largest part; secondary controls are smaller keys. depth: same material height as everything else; loudness comes from hue, never from extra elevation. colour: orange appears once per unit, on the primary control's body only; links, borders, icons, selections and charts never use it. type: not affected (P5 decides type). motion: the loud control is the only part whose response may carry the spring's settle; secondary keys press and return without settle. interaction: the loud control is first in the unit's tab order after the display's label.

### P2 The display tells the state
Rams asks that good design make a product understandable. Every unit has one window that says what the device is doing now, in numbers or a few words, and nothing else in the unit repeats it. **Therefore** space: the display window sits at the top of the unit, aligned to the body's inner inset. shape: the window is a rounded rectangle whose radius nests in the body (inner = outer − inset). depth: the window is recessed into the body (inverse light). colour: two display families, LCD (dark grey segments on grey-green, ≥4.5:1) and OLED (white on near-black). type: tabular numerals, a 7-segment face for LCD values, the system grotesk for OLED text. motion: values change at once; only a segment's on/off fades, ≤80ms. interaction: the display is a live region (polite) and never a control.

### P3 Parts behave like parts
Rams asks that good design be honest and thorough down to the last detail. A control in dial is a key, rocker, rotary, fader or slide switch, and it moves like that mechanism and nothing else. **Therefore** space: every part keeps ≥44px hit area even when it draws smaller. shape: keys are round or capsule; rotaries are circles with one index mark; faders ride a groove with a ruler scale. depth: press drops height to 0 in 60–70ms, return runs on the material's spring (ζ 0.75–1, settle <600ms). colour: not affected (P1 decides colour). type: scale numerals on a fader are tabular, the smallest size step, ink at ≥4.5:1. motion: fast in, slow out, through the state motion tokens; rotaries and faders settle into the nearest detent; under Reduce motion nothing overshoots and detents change at once with a short fade. interaction: every mechanism has its keyboard map (arrows step a detent, Home and End reach the ends, Space and Enter press) and a visible value and label.

### P4 Holes are information
Rams asks for as little design as possible. A perforated grille appears only where it carries a reading, such as a level, activity or a signal, and the dots themselves are the meter. **Therefore** space: grille pitch is one module step, so holes sit on the same grid as the body's inset. shape: holes are circles; their field is a rectangle or circle that nests in the body. depth: each hole is a recess (inverse light), never a printed dot. colour: holes are ink at a lower step; a lit level uses ink at full step, never orange. type: not affected (no text in a grille). motion: a level change fills holes in reading order, ≤120ms, none under Reduce motion. interaction: a grille that shows a value exposes it as text to assistive tech; a decorative grille is not allowed to exist.

### P5 Type is a label, not a voice
Rams asks that good design be aesthetic only through usefulness. One grotesk, few sizes. The wordmark sits bottom-left, the model name in lowercase bottom-right, like printing on a case. **Therefore** space: labels sit on the body's inset line, never floating mid-surface. shape: not affected (P3 decides shape). depth: printing is flat on the material, with no shadow and no emboss. colour: ink on plastic at ≥4.5:1 in both themes; no tinted text. type: one grotesk, regular and medium only; hierarchy by size and position; lowercase model names; tabular figures everywhere a number changes. motion: text does not animate. interaction: labels name their control through aria-labelledby, and the model name is never the only identifier.

### P6 Lasts, so it stays still
Rams asks that good design be long-lasting and kind to the environment. dial does not chase attention. Surfaces stay put; only a state change moves anything. **Therefore** space: fixed positions, so a unit's layout does not reflow between states. shape: radius ≈ 15–18% of the body's short side (estimated from chat, re-measure from ref-01.png), capped so large panels do not swell into pillows. depth: shadows are short and soft; a unit never floats more than its material height. colour: no gradients, no glow except P2's emitters. type: not affected (P5 decides type). motion: no entry choreography, no idle animation; transitions only on state. interaction: no hover lift; hover only darkens a key's ink a step, because a real key does not rise when you approach it.

## Light model


One light, top-left, for every part in every theme. A raised part (body, key, knob cap) casts a short soft shadow down and right, offset = height × light vector, and carries a faint lit hairline on its top-left edge (matte ABS, gloss ~0.2). A recess (display window, fader groove, grille hole) is the inverse: shade on its top-left inner edge, a faint lit edge bottom-right. A pressed part drops to height 0 and its shadow collapses under it. The chosen or "on" item is not lit brighter; it is the one in orange or the one whose indicator is on. Disabled parts lose height (flat, no shadow) and keep ≥3:1 for their outline. **What emits:** only the displays (LCD segments do not emit, OLED text does) and the small status lamp; in dark theme nothing else glows, and no white part glows because it is white.

Material row (tactile recipe): **matte ABS** — height 5px, gloss 0.2, roughness 3px, mass 1.4. Accent material: the same ABS dyed orange; it has the same height and light, never a different finish.
