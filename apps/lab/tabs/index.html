Tabs — three ways to choose a section of a screen

Tabs move between sections (rooms); they do not change a setting on the device. So they should not look like a key that does something. One light, matte ABS, ink on plastic, never orange. A prints the room names on the case like a scale; B reuses SegmentedControl's latching keys; C is a radio tuning scale with a sliding needle.

A · printed names, ink index mark under the chosen one

Living roomHeater 21.5 °C · Lamp 60%

Printing, not a part: no height, no travel. The chosen name is medium weight with a short 4px ink index mark under it, like the mark on a rotary. The mark appears at once under the new name; it never slides, and there is no rule across the row.

B · latching keys (the SegmentedControl bank)

Living roomHeater 21.5 °C · Lamp 60%

Reads as a mode switch on the device, so a reader expects pressing Kitchen to change the heater. It cannot be told apart from SegmentedControl.

C · tuning scale, needle on the chosen name

Living roomHeater 21.5 °C · Lamp 60%

A radio dial scale. The sliding needle is a moving indicator between sections, the same gesture as the sliding underline it was meant to avoid, and it moves on navigation, not on a state change.
